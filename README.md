## What 1 GB of RAM actually handles

1 GB sits at the bottom of the useful range. The common guide is 1 GB for testing and tiny sites, 2 GB for a small WordPress install, and 4 GB or more for WooCommerce or several sites on one machine. Treat that as the floor rather than an aspiration.

It comfortably runs a static or low-traffic site, a staging copy of a production site, a small API or chat bot, a couple of Docker containers, a private proxy, or a file drop. It does not run WooCommerce, several WordPress installs, or a control panel plus database plus site on the same box without swapping and long waits. Very limited resources slow down fast once WordPress, ecommerce or a database is added on top.

If you do buy the 1 GB plan, keep the stack light: Nginx with MariaDB instead of a heavier web stack, Redis or Memcached for caching, htop or Glances to watch CPU and memory, security patches applied promptly, and unused services disabled. That is the difference between a $1.83 box that serves pages and one that times out under a traffic spike. 👉 [See what the 1 GB plan includes](https://bit.ly/RacKnerd)

## When a cheap VPS is the wrong purchase

Skip it if you would rather not administer a server at all. Unmanaged means the operating system, the firewall, the updates and the backups are your job, and a $2 plan does not come with someone to do them.

Skip it if the site gets a handful of visitors a day. Shared hosting covers that for less effort and less money.

Skip it if your application needs a Windows-only stack, if you need the isolation of a physical server, or if you want fully managed hosting with someone else on call. Those start above this bracket, and squeezing them into $5/mo produces a bad experience rather than a saving.

There is a reliability side too. Cheap plans tend to share CPU cores harder and to include less support, and price by itself says nothing about uptime. Whatever you pay, keep your own off-server backups, because the provider's backup policy on an unmanaged annual plan is rarely the safety net people assume.
